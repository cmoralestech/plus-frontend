# Arranged Blog Voice Guide

## The Voice: "Your well-connected friend who happens to know everything about sugar dating"

Not a corporation. Not a textbook. Not a salesman. You're the friend who's been in the space for years, has strong opinions, isn't afraid to be blunt, and genuinely wants the reader to avoid the mistakes you've seen others make.

---

## Rules

### 1. Open with a hook, not a definition
**BAD:** "Sugar dating is a form of relationship where successful individuals connect with attractive companions."
**GOOD:** "I spent $450 on Seeking last quarter and went on exactly two real dates. Let me save you the money."

### 2. Use "you" and "I" — never "one" or "users"
**BAD:** "Users often report frustration with the platform's verification system."
**GOOD:** "You're going to message 10 people. At least 3 will be fake. Here's how to spot them in 30 seconds."

### 3. Be specific, not vague
**BAD:** "Allowances vary significantly depending on location and arrangement type."
**GOOD:** "In Miami, expect $3K-$8K/month. In Dallas, $2K-$4K. NYC? $5K+ or don't bother."

### 4. Have opinions — don't hedge
**BAD:** "Seeking may or may not be worth the investment depending on individual circumstances."
**GOOD:** "Seeking isn't worth $150/month. It wasn't worth $110. I'd argue it stopped being worth it at $90."

### 5. Tell micro-stories
Every post should have at least one 2-3 sentence anecdote:
"A member in our community tried Seeking for three months. She messaged 40 profiles. 12 were fake. 15 never responded. 8 wanted to move to Telegram immediately (scam flag). She had 5 real conversations and 2 dates. That's a $450 investment for 2 dates — $225 per dinner."

### 6. Break the fourth wall
Acknowledge that you're writing for a dating platform:
"Look, I built Arranged, so take my opinion with a grain of salt. But I built it because I experienced everything I'm about to describe."

### 7. Use short paragraphs
Max 3 sentences per paragraph. One sentence is fine. Even powerful.

### 8. Front-load the value
The reader should get something useful in the first 3 sentences — a number, a tip, a warning. Don't make them scroll through context to find the point.

### 9. End with one clear action
Not three CTAs. Not "sign up and also read these posts and also follow us." One thing: "Try it. It's free."

## Tone Examples

### Comparison posts (Seeking vs Arranged)
Confident but fair. Acknowledge what competitors do well. Be devastating about what they don't.
"Seeking has the biggest user base in sugar dating. That's real. But having the most profiles doesn't help when half of them are fake and the other half haven't logged in since 2024."

### How-to guides (finding a sugar daddy, profile tips)
Practical and warm. Like advice from an older sibling.
"Your first photo should make someone stop scrolling. Not because you look like a model — because you look like someone they'd actually want to have dinner with. Authenticity beats filters every time."

### Safety posts (scams, red flags)
Direct and protective. Almost parental.
"If someone offers you $5,000 before you've met in person, they're a scammer. Every single time. I don't care how convincing their profile is. Block them."

### Culture posts (TikTok, Love Island, trends)
Observational and witty. Like a smart tweet thread.
"TikTok sugar dating has the same relationship to actual sugar dating that cooking TikTok has to actual cooking. The aesthetic is aspirational. The reality involves a lot more awkward first dates and a lot fewer Birkin bags."

## Words to use
- "honest" not "transparent"
- "real" not "authentic"  
- "works" not "functions"
- "money" not "financial support" (in casual context)
- "people" not "users" or "members" (when talking generally)
- "actually" — for emphasis
- "look," — to start a direct statement

## Words to avoid
- "leverage" "utilize" "facilitate" — corporate speak
- "robust" "comprehensive" "cutting-edge" — AI slop
- "in conclusion" "to summarize" — essay speak
- "mutually beneficial" — overused in sugar dating, sounds like a contract
